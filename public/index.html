              How this works

              What the colors mean

              The decline-risk score runs from 0 to 100. It shows how likely a country's population is to shrink, based on five things: births per woman, the balance of births and deaths, how old the population is, whether migration fills the gap, and the UN's projected change to 2050 and 2100. It measures population-decline risk only. It is not a judgment of any country, its people, or their choices.

              LevelScoreMeaning

                Births per woman

                The average number of children a woman would have over her life at today's rates (demographers call this the total fertility rate, TFR). About 2.1 keeps a population steady over time. The extra 0.1 covers children who don't survive to adulthood.

                Recorded versus projected

                Solid lines are recorded estimates. Dashed lines in the shaded area are the UN World Population Prospects medium projection: the UN's central forecast, not a certainty. Real outcomes can land above or below it.

                Where growth comes from

                A population changes through births, deaths and net migration (people arriving minus people leaving). "Born abroad, living here" and "born here, living abroad" count people, not yearly moves.

                The 100-people model

                Each generation is today's births per woman ÷ 2.1, about 30 years apart. It deliberately ignores migration, longer lives and the age structure of today's population, so it shows the long-run pull of a birth rate, not a forecast. The UN projection is the forecast.

                Small places

                Places with fewer than 100,000 people, or with missing inputs, get no rank. When they have enough data they still get a score. A few births either way can swing a small place's numbers a lot from one year to the next.
